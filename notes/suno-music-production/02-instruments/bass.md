# Bass

> **Pillar:** 2 — Instrument Technique · **Last updated:** 2026-08-06
> **Suno version notes:** version-independent technique; Suno-translation section verified against Suno v5.5, 2026-08

## Core Concept

Bass's primary job is **locking with the kick drum** — playing the rhythmic pattern that reinforces the low end the drummer's kick is establishing, so the rhythm section reads as one unified pulse rather than two competing parts.

**Root-following vs. walking**: the simplest bass part just plays each chord's root note for its duration ("root-following"). A more sophisticated part adds **approach notes** in the last beat(s) before a chord change — a scalar step (moving stepwise through the scale toward the next root) or a chromatic step (moving by a half-step, outside the scale, purely as a connecting tone) that leads the ear into the next chord. This "walking" motion is what makes a bass line feel intentional rather than just static roots.

## Practical Application

- **Verse**: root notes in steady eighth notes, locked tightly to the kick pattern — see [drums.md](drums.md) — for a driving, hypnotic feel.
- **Approach-note logic into changes**: e.g., moving from an Em chord to a C chord, a scalar approach walks E-D-C in the bar's last beats; moving chromatically from C to D walks C-C#-D. These small connecting notes are what separate "a bassist" from "a sequencer playing roots."
- **Pre-chorus**: shifting the pattern to busier straight eighths (sometimes an octave up) adds urgency without changing the underlying harmony — a rhythmic build rather than a harmonic one.
- **Chorus**: often simplifies back to strong root notes, but adding an octave jump on a strong beat (e.g., beat 3) gives bounce and reinforces the chorus's "bigness."
- **Tone**: picked (bright, percussive, cuts through a dense mix) vs. fingered (warmer, rounder) — distortion/overdrive on bass adds grit and helps it cut through a wall of guitars.

## Suno Translation

Style-field tags: `driving root-note bass`, `walking bass`, `overdriven bass`, `punchy bass`. Because bass is a supporting instrument rather than a lead one, it rarely needs more than 1-2 tag slots — spend more budget on guitar/vocal/drums, which shape the identity of the track more.

Judging output: listen for whether the bass and kick drum feel locked together (same rhythmic pulse) — if the bass feels disconnected from the drum pattern, that's a mix-cohesion issue worth a regeneration rather than a tag-wording problem.
